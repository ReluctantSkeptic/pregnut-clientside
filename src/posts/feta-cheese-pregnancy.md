**The short answer:** Yes, you can eat feta while pregnant when it is **made with pasteurized milk**. [CDC's pregnancy food-safety chart](https://www.cdc.gov/food-safety/foods/pregnant-women.html) names **feta**, along with cottage cheese, cream cheese, string cheese, and mozzarella, as a safer choice when made with pasteurized milk. Soft cheese made from **unpasteurized (raw) milk** is on CDC's riskier list.

## Why do some lists say to avoid feta when pregnant?

Because feta is not always pasteurized. The [FDA explains](https://www.fda.gov/food/people-risk-foodborne-illness/fact-or-fiction-food-safety-moms-be) that some soft cheeses may be made with unpasteurized milk, especially traditional, homemade soft cheeses, and that cheese made from unpasteurized milk can become contaminated with *Listeria*. FDA's advice is not to eat soft cheeses such as feta **unless** they are made with pasteurized milk. That "unless" is often dropped from short lists, which is why feta shows up as a blanket "avoid."

## How do I know if feta is pasteurized?

Read the label for "made with pasteurized milk." [FDA notes](https://www.fda.gov/food/people-risk-foodborne-illness/dairy-and-eggs-food-safety-moms-be) that milk sold across state lines is pasteurized, but some cheeses are not, especially those produced and sold locally on farms or at local cheese shops. For feta from a farmers' market, an imported specialty shop, or a restaurant, ask, and skip it if no one can confirm. Our [pasteurized vs unpasteurized cheese section](/blog/soft-cheese-pregnancy/) walks through the label check.

## Can you eat crumbled feta in a salad?

Yes, when the feta was made with pasteurized milk. Packaged feta crumbles carry the same milk statement as a block, so check it the same way. Wash the greens too: CDC lists unwashed leafy greens as riskier; see our [produce washing guide](/blog/wash-produce-pregnancy/).

## Is baked or cooked feta safe during pregnancy?

Heating adds a margin. CDC's chart lists **pasteurized soft cheeses heated to 165°F or until steaming hot** as a safer choice, and [CDC's Listeria prevention page](https://www.cdc.gov/listeria/prevention/index.html) also lists raw-milk cheeses heated to 165°F or until steaming hot among its safer options. Baked feta pasta, spanakopita, or feta melted into a hot dish fits that when the cheese is steaming hot throughout.

## What about goat cheese and halloumi?

CDC's chart does not name goat cheese or halloumi, so use the same two checks: the label should say **made with pasteurized milk**, and soft cheeses heated until steaming hot are the more cautious option. Compare nutrients for [soft goat cheese](/food/01159-cheese-goat-soft-type/) and [semisoft goat cheese](/food/01157-cheese-goat-semisoft-type/) on PregNut.

## What nutrients does feta provide?

PregNut's historical USDA-based data lists [feta](/food/01019-cheese-feta/) at **493 mg calcium**, **14.21 g protein**, and **1.69 mcg vitamin B12** per 100 g. Our [calcium without dairy guide](/blog/calcium-without-dairy-pregnancy/) compares other calcium foods.

## Common questions

### What if I accidentally ate unpasteurized feta while pregnant?

An exposure does not mean you have an infection. Tell your prenatal care team what you ate. [CDC says](https://www.cdc.gov/food-safety/signs-symptoms/index.html) pregnant women should call the doctor right away if they have a fever and feel more tired and achy than usual.

### Is feta safe in the first trimester?

CDC's chart does not set different cheese rules by trimester. Pasteurized feta is on its safer list throughout pregnancy.

**The short answer:** Iodine helps the body make thyroid hormones needed for fetal brain development. The [NIH pregnancy recommended dietary allowance](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Iodine-Consumer/) is **220 micrograms (mcg) a day**. If you eat little or no dairy, seafood, or eggs, check your prenatal's iodine amount with your care team rather than assuming those foods have been replaced.

## Which foods and labels matter?

- **Eggs** can contribute iodine if you eat them. For pregnancy food safety, follow [CDC advice to cook eggs until the whites and yolks are firm](https://www.cdc.gov/food-safety/foods/pregnant-women.html).
- **Iodized salt** contains iodine, but sea salt, kosher salt, and salt in most processed foods usually are not iodized. Check the package. This is a label distinction, not a reason to add extra salt to meals.
- **Seaweed** can contain iodine, but amounts vary greatly among species. Do not treat an unmeasured serving or kelp supplement as a predictable daily dose.
- **Plant-based milk** is not automatically an iodine source. Check whether a product explicitly lists added iodine; a calcium-fortified carton does not establish its iodine content.

These distinctions come from the [NIH iodine fact sheets](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Iodine-Consumer/) and [pregnancy supplement review](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Pregnancy-HealthProfessional/).

## What should I check on my prenatal?

Find **iodine** on the Supplement Facts panel, then read the amount **per serving** and the serving size. Not every prenatal contains iodine. If you avoid several common sources, discuss the product and your usual diet with your prenatal clinician before adding another supplement; too much iodine can also affect the thyroid. Our [prenatal label guide](/blog/prenatal-vitamin-label-pregnancy/) shows how to compare the rest of the label.

**A limit of PregNut's food lookup:** Its current food-composition dataset does **not** include iodine values. A food page cannot tell you what percentage of the iodine recommendation it supplies. Use product labels and the [NIH iodine resources](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Iodine-HealthProfessional/) for this question.

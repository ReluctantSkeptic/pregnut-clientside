**The short answer:** [CDC lists refrigerated smoked seafood as a riskier choice during pregnancy](https://www.cdc.gov/food-safety/foods/pregnant-women.html), unless it is in a cooked dish. This includes refrigerated smoked salmon sold as **lox** or **nova-style** fish. CDC lists smoked fish in a sealed, shelf-stable package that **does not need refrigeration before opening** as a safer choice. Check the actual product and how you plan to serve it.

## Is lox on a bagel different from cooked salmon?

Yes. Lox and other refrigerated smoked fish are generally served cold, without a final cooking step. [FDA's pregnancy food-safety guidance](https://www.fda.gov/food/people-risk-foodborne-illness/ready-eat-foods-food-safety-moms-be) advises avoiding refrigerated smoked seafood unless it is in a cooked dish, such as a casserole. A toasted bagel does not cook the fish placed on top of it.

For a cooked fish meal, [CDC lists fish cooked to **145°F**, or until the flesh is opaque and separates easily with a fork, as a safer choice](https://www.cdc.gov/food-safety/foods/pregnant-women.html). If using refrigerated smoked fish in a dish, cook the dish thoroughly rather than adding the fish after cooking.

## What should I check on the package?

- **Does it require refrigeration before opening?** Refrigerated smoked fish falls under CDC's riskier category when eaten without cooking. “Smoked,” “lox,” “nova-style,” and “kippered” can appear on these packages; the word “smoked” alone does not establish shelf stability.
- **Is it sealed and shelf-stable before opening?** CDC lists this as a safer smoked-fish option. Follow the package's storage directions after opening.
- **Which fish is it?** Food-safety preparation and mercury are separate checks. Use the [FDA/EPA fish chart](https://www.fda.gov/food/consumers/advice-about-eating-fish) to identify the species and serving guidance.

PregNut's [smoked whitefish nutrient entry](/food/15131-whitefish-smoked/) reports nutrients for a reference food; it cannot tell whether a product is refrigerated, shelf-stable, or heated. Our [lower-mercury fish guide](/blog/low-mercury-fish-pregnancy/) explains fish choices and weekly servings separately from this smoked-seafood handling question.

## How do you cook smoked salmon when pregnant?

Cook it **in** the dish. [CDC's Listeria prevention page](https://www.cdc.gov/listeria/prevention/index.html) lists smoked fish cooked in a casserole or other cooked dishes as a safer choice, and [FDA's example](https://www.fda.gov/food/people-risk-foodborne-illness/ready-eat-foods-food-safety-moms-be) is a casserole. Mix refrigerated smoked salmon into a baked pasta, potato bake, frittata, or quiche before it goes in the oven, and cook the dish through. Smoked salmon laid on top of a hot dish as a garnish has not been cooked.

## Common questions

### Can I eat smoked salmon in the first trimester?

CDC's chart does not change by trimester. Refrigerated smoked salmon is on its riskier list throughout pregnancy unless it is in a cooked dish.

### What if I ate smoked salmon or lox while pregnant?

An exposure does not mean you have an infection. Tell your prenatal care team what you ate. [CDC's symptom table](https://www.cdc.gov/food-safety/signs-symptoms/index.html) lists smoked fish among common sources of *Listeria* and says pregnant women should call the doctor right away if they have a fever and feel more tired and achy than usual.

### Is hot-smoked salmon different from cold-smoked?

CDC and FDA do not sort smoked fish by smoking method. Their checks are whether it is refrigerated or shelf-stable, and whether it is cooked in a dish. [FDA lists](https://www.fda.gov/food/people-risk-foodborne-illness/ready-eat-foods-food-safety-moms-be) "kippered" and "smoked" among the labels used on refrigerated smoked seafood.

### Is canned salmon OK?

Yes. [CDC lists canned fish and seafood](https://www.cdc.gov/food-safety/foods/pregnant-women.html) as a safer choice.

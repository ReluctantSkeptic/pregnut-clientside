**The short answer:** [CDC lists refrigerated smoked seafood as a riskier choice during pregnancy](https://www.cdc.gov/food-safety/foods/pregnant-women.html), unless it is in a cooked dish. This includes refrigerated smoked salmon sold as **lox** or **nova-style** fish. CDC lists smoked fish in a sealed, shelf-stable package that **does not need refrigeration before opening** as a safer choice. Check the actual product and how you plan to serve it.

## Is lox on a bagel different from cooked salmon?

Yes. Lox and other refrigerated smoked fish are generally served cold, without a final cooking step. [FDA's pregnancy food-safety guidance](https://www.fda.gov/food/people-risk-foodborne-illness/ready-eat-foods-food-safety-moms-be) advises avoiding refrigerated smoked seafood unless it is in a cooked dish, such as a casserole. A toasted bagel does not cook the fish placed on top of it.

For a cooked fish meal, [CDC lists fish cooked to **145°F**, or until the flesh is opaque and separates easily with a fork, as a safer choice](https://www.cdc.gov/food-safety/foods/pregnant-women.html). If using refrigerated smoked fish in a dish, cook the dish thoroughly rather than adding the fish after cooking.

## What should I check on the package?

- **Does it require refrigeration before opening?** Refrigerated smoked fish falls under CDC's riskier category when eaten without cooking. “Smoked,” “lox,” “nova-style,” and “kippered” can appear on these packages; the word “smoked” alone does not establish shelf stability.
- **Is it sealed and shelf-stable before opening?** CDC lists this as a safer smoked-fish option. Follow the package's storage directions after opening.
- **Which fish is it?** Food-safety preparation and mercury are separate checks. Use the [FDA/EPA fish chart](https://www.fda.gov/food/consumers/advice-about-eating-fish) to identify the species and serving guidance.

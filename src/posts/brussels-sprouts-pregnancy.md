**The short answer:** Yes, you can eat Brussels sprouts while pregnant. They are a vegetable, not the germinated seed sprouts, such as alfalfa and bean sprouts, that the [CDC lists as a riskier choice](https://www.cdc.gov/food-safety/foods/pregnant-women.html) during pregnancy. Wash them before eating; the CDC notes that washed and then cooked vegetables are the safest choice.

## Why can't pregnant women eat sprouts, and does that include Brussels sprouts?

The pregnancy warning is about **raw or undercooked seed sprouts**. The [CDC's safer food chart](https://www.cdc.gov/food-safety/foods/pregnant-women.html) lists raw alfalfa and bean sprouts as riskier and names sprouts cooked **until steaming hot** as the safer choice. Our [raw and cooked sprouts guide](/blog/sprouts-during-pregnancy/) explains why rinsing does not fix that problem.

Brussels sprouts share the word "sprouts" (and are often spelled "brussel sprouts"), but they are not germinated seeds. The steaming-hot sprout rule does not apply to them. The CDC's general vegetable advice does.

## Can you eat raw Brussels sprouts while pregnant?

The CDC places **unwashed** fresh vegetables in its riskier column and **washed** vegetables in its safer column, adding that washed and then cooked produce is safest. Shaved raw Brussels sprouts in a salad fit the safer column when they are washed well; roasted, steamed, or sautéed sprouts fit the safest one. See [how to wash fruits and vegetables when pregnant](/blog/wash-produce-pregnancy/) for the steps.

At a restaurant, you cannot check how a raw salad was washed, so a cooked side is the simpler choice.

## How much folate is in Brussels sprouts?

The [NIH folate fact sheet](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Folate-HealthProfessional/) names Brussels sprouts among the foods with the highest folate levels. It lists **78 mcg DFE** for ½ cup of frozen, boiled Brussels sprouts. The pregnancy recommendation in the same fact sheet is **600 mcg DFE a day**.

PregNut's historical USDA-based data lists these amounts per **100 g**:

- [Boiled Brussels sprouts](/food/11099-brussels-sprouts-boiled/): **60 mcg DFE** folate, **62 mg** vitamin C, and **317 mg** potassium.
- [Raw Brussels sprouts](/food/11098-brussels-sprouts-raw/): **61 mcg DFE** folate and **85 mg** vitamin C.

For comparison, [NIH lists 85 mg of vitamin C a day](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminC-Consumer/) for pregnant adults. Food folate helps, but it is not the same as folic acid. The [CDC recommends 400 mcg of folic acid daily](https://www.cdc.gov/folic-acid/about/index.html) for anyone who could become pregnant, from a supplement or fortified foods. Our [folate and folic acid guide](/blog/folate-folic-acid-pregnancy/) explains the difference.

## Are Brussels sprouts OK in the first trimester?

Yes. The CDC's pregnancy food chart does not set different rules by trimester, so the same washing and cooking advice applies from early pregnancy on. If nausea makes strong-smelling vegetables hard to face, our [morning sickness food guide](/blog/foods-for-morning-sickness-pregnancy/) has gentler options.

## Common questions

### Are "brussel sprouts" and Brussels sprouts the same?

Yes. "Brussel sprouts" is a common spelling of the same vegetable, and the same guidance applies.

### What if I ate raw Brussels sprouts while pregnant?

Washed raw vegetables are on the CDC's safer-choice list. If you feel unwell after any meal, the [CDC says to see a doctor if you are pregnant and have a fever with other flu-like symptoms](https://www.cdc.gov/food-safety/signs-symptoms/index.html).

### Can Brussels sprouts replace my prenatal vitamin?

No. Vegetables add food folate, but the CDC's 400 mcg folic acid recommendation is met with a supplement or fortified foods. Check your [prenatal vitamin label](/blog/prenatal-vitamin-label-pregnancy/).

**The short answer:** Yes, you can eat sour cream while pregnant when it is made from **pasteurized** milk or cream and has been kept cold. The [CDC lists pasteurized milk and dairy products made from pasteurized milk](https://www.cdc.gov/food-safety/foods/pregnant-women.html) as the safer choice, and raw-milk dairy products as the riskier one.

## Is store-bought sour cream pasteurized?

Check the label. The [FDA's dairy advice for moms-to-be](https://www.fda.gov/food/people-risk-foodborne-illness/dairy-and-eggs-food-safety-moms-be) explains that milk sold across state lines must be pasteurized, but some dairy products, especially some cheeses, are not, so reading the label is the reliable step. Look for the word **"pasteurized"** in the ingredients or on the package, and be more careful with homemade or farm-stand dairy. Our [raw milk guide](/blog/raw-milk-pregnancy/) covers what the label wording means.

The same check applies to **crema** and other cultured creams: choose ones labeled as made with pasteurized milk or cream.

## How long can sour cream sit out?

Keep it refrigerated at **40°F or below**, as FDA advises for milk products. At a taco bar, buffet, or picnic, FDA's [two-hour rule](https://www.fda.gov/food/people-risk-foodborne-illness/ready-eat-foods-food-safety-moms-be) applies: do not eat perishable food that has been left out for more than **2 hours**, or more than **1 hour** when it is above 90°F. A bowl of sour cream that sat out through a long party is the one to skip.

## Is sour cream on cooked food OK?

Yes. A spoonful of pasteurized sour cream on a baked potato, chili, or tacos is fine. It does not need to be heated, unlike [queso fresco](/blog/queso-fresco-pregnancy/), which the CDC treats differently even when pasteurized.

## Does sour cream have any pregnancy nutrients?

Some calcium. PregNut's historical USDA-based data lists about **141 mg calcium** per 100 g for both [light sour cream](/food/01179-sour-cream-light/) and [reduced-fat sour cream](/food/01178-sour-cream-reduced-fat/). A typical spoonful is much smaller than 100 g, so it is a minor source. Our [calcium guide](/blog/calcium-without-dairy-pregnancy/) compares bigger contributors. [Fat-free sour cream](/food/01180-sour-cream-fat-free/) has its own entry.

## Common questions

### What about sour cream and onion dip?

Store-bought dips made with pasteurized dairy follow the same rules: check the label and keep them cold. Apply the two-hour rule at parties.

### Is Greek yogurt a substitute for sour cream?

It can be, if it is pasteurized. The CDC's safer-choice list includes [pasteurized yogurt](https://www.cdc.gov/listeria/prevention/index.html).

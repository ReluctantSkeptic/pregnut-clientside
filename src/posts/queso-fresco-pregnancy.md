**The short answer:** Avoid **unheated** queso fresco while pregnant, even if the label says pasteurized. The [CDC lists any queso fresco-type cheese](https://www.cdc.gov/food-safety/foods/pregnant-women.html), pasteurized or not, as a riskier choice. Its safer choice is queso fresco **heated to 165°F or until steaming hot**, such as cheese melted inside a hot dish.

## Why is pasteurized queso fresco still a concern?

The [FDA's queso fresco guidance](https://www.fda.gov/food/buy-store-serve-safe-food/queso-fresco-type-cheeses-consumer-guidance) says most queso fresco sold in stores is pasteurized, but the cheese can be **recontaminated** with *Listeria* after pasteurization if the plant is not clean. These soft, moist, low-acid cheeses can support *Listeria* growth, and *Listeria* can grow in the refrigerator. FDA reports that listeriosis outbreaks have repeatedly been linked to queso fresco-type cheeses and recommends that people at higher risk, including pregnant women, avoid them.

FDA also notes that pregnant women are about **10 times** more likely than the general population to get listeriosis. Our [soft cheese guide](/blog/soft-cheese-pregnancy/) explains how other cheeses, like feta and mozzarella, compare.

## What queso fresco can pregnant women eat?

Queso fresco that has been **cooked as part of the dish** to 165°F or until steaming hot. FDA's guidance suggests cooking it in the dish when you can. Examples where the cheese is likely to be heated through, if the dish is served steaming hot:

- Baked enchiladas or casseroles with queso fresco melted inside.
- [Pupusas con queso](/food/36409-latino-pupusas-con-queso-pupusas-cheese/) cooked on the griddle until the filling is hot.
- Cheese dip served bubbling hot.

Cheese **crumbled on top** after cooking, such as on tacos, elote, beans, or salads, has not been heated and stays in the riskier column.

## Is queso blanco or panela the same?

The CDC and FDA group **queso blanco, requesón**, and similar fresh, soft cheeses with queso fresco. FDA's moms-to-be page also names panela alongside them. Treat them the same way: heat them until steaming hot.

## Is restaurant queso fresco safe while pregnant?

Ask whether the cheese is cooked into the dish or added cold at the end. Request crumbled cheese on the side or left off, and choose dishes where the cheese is melted and the plate arrives steaming hot.

## If I buy queso fresco, how should I handle it?

FDA advises choosing cheese in sealed packaging that is clearly labeled as made with pasteurized milk, with a use-by date and the producer's name. Keep it at **40°F or below**, use it soon, do not keep leftovers after opening, and discard it if it sat between 40°F and 140°F for more than **2 hours**. Wash your hands, knives, and boards after handling it.

## Common questions

### What if I ate queso fresco while pregnant?

The [CDC says pregnant women should call the doctor right away](https://www.cdc.gov/food-safety/signs-symptoms/index.html) if they have a fever and feel more tired and achy than usual. FDA notes that listeriosis in pregnancy can be mild or unnoticed, so tell your prenatal care team what you ate even if you feel fine.

### Is homemade queso fresco safer?

The FDA's dairy advice for moms-to-be is to use **pasteurized milk** if you make queso fresco at home, and the CDC's heat-it-first rule still applies.

**The short answer:** A **fortified** breakfast cereal can be a useful pregnancy food because it can add folic acid and iron. The [CDC recommends 400 mcg of folic acid daily](https://www.cdc.gov/folic-acid/about/index.html) for anyone who could become pregnant and names fortified breakfast cereals as one source. Check the Nutrition Facts label, because fortification varies widely between cereals.

## What cereal is good for pregnancy?

Look at three lines on the label:

- **Folate.** Labels list folate in mcg DFE, with the folic acid amount in parentheses when it is added. The [NIH folate fact sheet](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Folate-HealthProfessional/) uses a cereal fortified with **25% of the Daily Value**, or **100 mcg DFE** per serving, as an example; some cereals are fortified to 100%. The pregnancy recommendation is **600 mcg DFE** a day from all sources.
- **Iron.** The [NIH iron fact sheet](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Iron-HealthProfessional/) lists **18 mg** of iron in a serving of cereal fortified with 100% of the Daily Value. The pregnancy recommendation is **27 mg** a day.
- **Fiber and added sugar.** These vary by cereal; compare labels if constipation or blood sugar is on your mind. Our [fiber guide](/blog/fiber-foods-constipation-pregnancy/) has more ideas.

## Is Special K or corn flakes good for pregnancy?

Both are fortified cereals in PregNut's historical USDA-based dataset. Per **100 g** (several servings), the data lists:

- [Special K](/food/08067-cereal-kellogg-s-special-k/): **28 mg** iron and **2,180 mcg DFE** folate.
- [Corn flakes](/food/08020-cereal-kellogg-s-corn-flakes/): **28.9 mg** iron and **583 mcg DFE** folate.
- [Cheerios](/food/08013-cereal-cheerios/): **33.17 mg** iron and **1,201 mcg DFE** folate.

These are historical reference values, not current product labels. Recipes and fortification change, so use the box in your hand for the serving amount.

## Can fortified cereal replace a prenatal vitamin?

Not on its own. Cereal can help, but the CDC's folic acid recommendation can be met with a supplement, fortified foods, or both, and a prenatal may also supply iron and other nutrients. Count cereal **alongside** your prenatal rather than doubling up blindly. The NIH folate fact sheet sets an adult upper limit of **1,000 mcg** of folic acid a day from supplements and fortified foods combined. Some cereals also add **vitamin A**; the [NIH vitamin A fact sheet](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminA-Consumer/) warns that too much preformed vitamin A during pregnancy can cause birth defects. Bring your cereal and prenatal labels to an appointment if the totals look high. See [how to read a prenatal label](/blog/prenatal-vitamin-label-pregnancy/).

## Is cereal good for nausea?

If nausea makes full meals hard, [ACOG suggests](https://www.acog.org/womens-health/faqs/morning-sickness-nausea-and-vomiting-of-pregnancy) eating frequent small bites. Dry cereal is easy to keep at hand. Our [morning sickness food guide](/blog/foods-for-morning-sickness-pregnancy/) has more options.

## Common questions

### Is Honey Bunches of Oats OK while pregnant?

Yes, it is a breakfast cereal, so the same label checks apply. PregNut has an entry for [Honey Bunches of Oats](/food/43570-cereal-honey-bunches-of-oats-honey-roasted/); compare its iron, folate, and sugar with other cereals.

### What about cereal with gestational diabetes?

Ask your care team for a carbohydrate plan. Compare total carbohydrate, fiber, and added sugar on the label rather than relying on a cereal's name.

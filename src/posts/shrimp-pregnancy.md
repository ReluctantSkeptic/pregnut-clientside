**The short answer:** Yes, you can eat shrimp while pregnant when it is fully cooked. Shrimp is on the [FDA's Best Choices list](https://www.fda.gov/food/consumers/advice-about-eating-fish) for mercury, and FDA's pregnancy advice is **2 to 3 servings a week** from that list, with one serving equal to **4 ounces**. Raw or undercooked shrimp, as in ceviche or some sushi, is on the [CDC's riskier list](https://www.cdc.gov/food-safety/foods/pregnant-women.html).

## How much shrimp can I eat while pregnant?

FDA does not set a separate shrimp limit. Its advice is **2 to 3 servings a week in total** from a variety of Best Choices, which include shrimp, salmon, cod, crab, and canned light tuna. One 4-ounce serving of shrimp plus one 4-ounce serving of salmon makes two servings for the week. The [Dietary Guidelines figure FDA cites](https://www.fda.gov/food/consumers/advice-about-eating-fish) is **8 to 12 ounces a week** of a variety of seafood lower in mercury.

Our [low-mercury fish guide](/blog/low-mercury-fish-pregnancy/) lists the Best Choices, Good Choices, and fish to avoid.

## How do I know shrimp is cooked?

The [FDA's seafood advice for moms-to-be](https://www.fda.gov/food/people-risk-foodborne-illness/meat-poultry-seafood-food-safety-moms-be) says to cook shrimp and lobster until the flesh is **opaque (milky white)**. The CDC similarly says to cook shellfish until the flesh is **pearly or white, and opaque**. Grilled, boiled, sautéed, and baked shrimp all work when the whole piece reaches that point.

## Can you eat cold shrimp or shrimp cocktail?

Cold shrimp fits the cooked-shellfish guidance when it was fully cooked before chilling. Ask or check the package to confirm the shrimp were cooked, keep it refrigerated, and follow FDA's rule not to leave perishable food out for more than **2 hours** (1 hour above 90°F). Skip shrimp that was "cooked" only in citrus juice, as in ceviche, because the CDC lists ceviche as a riskier choice.

## Can you eat crab while pregnant?

Yes, when it is cooked. Crab is also an FDA Best Choice, and it counts toward the same 2 to 3 weekly servings as shrimp. Crab legs, crab cakes, and blue crab follow the CDC's shellfish rule: cook until the flesh is pearly or white and opaque. PregNut's [cooked blue crab entry](/food/15140-crab-blue-cooked-moist-heat/) lists **17.88 g protein** and **3.33 mcg vitamin B12** per 100 g.

## What nutrients does shrimp provide?

PregNut's historical USDA-based data lists [cooked shrimp](/food/15151-shrimp-cooked/) at **22.78 g protein**, **135.4 mg choline**, and **0.14 g DHA** per 100 g. FDA notes that fish provide omega-3 fats (DHA and EPA), iron, iodine, and choline. See our [choline guide](/blog/choline-foods-pregnancy/) for how that compares with eggs and other foods.

## Common questions

### Are prawns the same as shrimp for pregnancy?

The CDC's shellfish rule applies to both: cook until the flesh is pearly or white and opaque. Count them toward your weekly seafood servings.

### What if I ate undercooked shrimp?

The [CDC says most people get vibriosis](https://www.cdc.gov/vibrio/about/index.html) by eating raw or undercooked shellfish, particularly oysters. If you are pregnant and have a fever with other flu-like symptoms, [see your doctor](https://www.cdc.gov/food-safety/signs-symptoms/index.html).

### Is shrimp high in mercury?

No. FDA places shrimp in its lowest-mercury group, Best Choices.

**The short answer:** Yes, you can eat salmon while pregnant when it is fully cooked. Salmon is on the [FDA's Best Choices list](https://www.fda.gov/food/consumers/advice-about-eating-fish) for mercury, and FDA's pregnancy advice is **2 to 3 servings a week** from that list, with one serving equal to **4 ounces**. [CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/food-safety/foods/pregnant-women.html) lists salmon among fish low in mercury and advises cooking fish to **145°F** or until the flesh is opaque and separates easily with a fork.

## Is salmon good for pregnancy?

Yes. [FDA says](https://www.fda.gov/food/consumers/advice-about-eating-fish) fish intake during pregnancy is recommended because moderate scientific evidence shows it can help a baby's cognitive development. FDA lists the nutrients fish provide during pregnancy as omega-3 fats (DHA and EPA), iron, iodine, and choline, plus protein, vitamin B12, vitamin D, and selenium.

Salmon is one of the richer sources of two of those. The [NIH omega-3 fact sheet](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Omega3FattyAcids-HealthProfessional/) lists **1.24 g DHA** in 3 ounces of cooked farmed Atlantic salmon. The [NIH vitamin D fact sheet](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminD-HealthProfessional/) lists **570 IU** in 3 ounces of cooked sockeye salmon; the pregnancy RDA in the same fact sheet is **600 IU (15 mcg) a day**.

## How much salmon can I eat while pregnant?

FDA does not set a separate salmon limit. Its advice is **2 to 3 servings a week in total** from a variety of Best Choices, with one serving equal to **4 ounces**. The [Dietary Guidelines figure FDA cites](https://www.fda.gov/food/consumers/advice-about-eating-fish) is **8 to 12 ounces a week** of a variety of seafood lower in mercury for people who are pregnant. Two 4-ounce salmon fillets plus one serving of [shrimp](/blog/shrimp-pregnancy/) would make three servings for the week.

## How should salmon be cooked when pregnant?

Cook it to **145°F**, or until the flesh is **opaque and separates easily with a fork**, per [CDC's pregnancy chart](https://www.cdc.gov/food-safety/foods/pregnant-women.html). [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/food/people-risk-foodborne-illness/meat-poultry-seafood-food-safety-moms-be) gives the same 145°F target for finfish. Baked, grilled, pan-seared, and poached salmon all work when the thickest part reaches that point. A fillet that is still translucent in the center is undercooked.

## What kind of salmon can you eat while pregnant?

- **Cooked fresh or frozen salmon:** a safer choice once it reaches 145°F.
- **Canned salmon:** CDC lists canned fish and seafood as a safer choice.
- **Smoked salmon and lox:** CDC lists refrigerated smoked seafood, often labeled "nova-style," "lox," "kippered," or "smoked," as riskier unless it is in a cooked dish. Shelf-stable smoked salmon in a sealed package that does not need refrigeration before opening is a safer choice. See our [smoked salmon guide](/blog/smoked-salmon-pregnancy/).
- **Raw salmon in sushi, sashimi, or poke:** CDC lists raw or undercooked fish as riskier. Our [sushi guide](/blog/sushi-pregnancy/) lists cooked rolls.

## Is farmed or wild salmon better in pregnancy?

FDA's chart lists **salmon** as a Best Choice without separating farmed from wild, so either fits the 2-to-3-servings advice. NIH's omega-3 table lists similar DHA in 3 ounces of cooked farmed (**1.24 g**) and wild (**1.22 g**) Atlantic salmon, and notes that the omega-3 content of farmed fish depends on what they are fed.

## Common questions

### Can I eat salmon in the first trimester?

Yes. FDA's fish advice applies to the whole pregnancy and does not set different amounts by trimester. The cooking advice is the same throughout.

### What if I ate undercooked or raw salmon?

Eating it once does not mean you have an infection. Tell your prenatal care team what you ate. The [CDC says pregnant women should call the doctor right away](https://www.cdc.gov/food-safety/signs-symptoms/index.html) if they have a fever and feel more tired and achy than usual.

### How does salmon compare with other fish?

Compare cooked [farmed Atlantic](/food/15237-salmon-atlantic-farmed-cooked-dry-heat/), [sockeye](/food/15086-salmon-sockeye-cooked-dry-heat/), and [canned pink salmon](/food/15084-salmon-pink-canned-total-can-contents/) on PregNut, and see our [lower-mercury fish guide](/blog/low-mercury-fish-pregnancy/) for the rest of FDA's Best Choices.

**The short answer:** Yes, most people can eat hard cheese while pregnant when it is made with **pasteurized milk**. [CDC's pregnancy food-safety chart](https://www.cdc.gov/food-safety/foods/pregnant-women.html) lists pasteurized hard cheeses such as **Asiago, Cheddar, Parmesan, and Swiss/Gruyère/Emmental** as safer choices. Any cheese made from raw (unpasteurized) milk is a riskier choice.

## Is cheddar safe during pregnancy?

Pasteurized cheddar is on CDC's safer list, by name. Check the label for "made with pasteurized milk." See our [cheddar](/food/01009-cheese-cheddar/) and [sharp sliced cheddar](/food/01270-cheese-cheddar-sharp-sliced/) entries for nutrition data.

## Can you eat Parmesan while pregnant?

Yes, when it is made with pasteurized milk. CDC names Parmesan among safer hard cheeses. Some imported or specialty Parmesan-style cheeses are made with raw milk, so the label is the deciding step: the [FDA notes](https://www.fda.gov/food/people-risk-foodborne-illness/dairy-and-eggs-food-safety-moms-be) that some cheeses are not made with pasteurized milk. [Parmesan](/food/01033-cheese-parmesan-hard/) has its own food page.

## Is Swiss cheese safe in pregnancy?

Pasteurized Swiss, Gruyère, and Emmental are on CDC's safer list. Check the label as with any cheese. Food pages: [Swiss](/food/01040-cheese-swiss/), [Gruyère](/food/01023-cheese-gruyere/), and [pasteurized process Swiss](/food/01044-cheese-pasteurized-process-swiss/).

## Can I eat provolone while pregnant?

Provolone is not named on CDC's list, so use the same two checks CDC applies to all cheese: is it **made with pasteurized milk**, and was it **sliced at a deli**? CDC lists **unheated cheese sliced at a deli** as a riskier choice, and deli-sliced cheese heated to **165°F or until steaming hot** as safer. Pre-packaged, pasteurized provolone avoids the deli-counter issue. A melted provolone sandwich that is steaming hot meets the heating step. See [provolone](/food/01035-cheese-provolone/).

## What about deli-sliced cheese?

CDC treats cheese sliced at the deli counter like deli meat: heat it to 165°F or until steaming hot. This applies to hard cheeses too.

## Which cheeses need extra care?

Soft and fresh cheeses follow different rules, especially queso fresco-type cheeses, which CDC lists as riskier even when pasteurized unless heated. Our [soft cheese guide](/blog/soft-cheese-pregnancy/) and [queso fresco guide](/blog/queso-fresco-pregnancy/) cover those.

## Common questions

### Does cooking hard cheese make it safer?

CDC lists cheeses heated to 165°F or until steaming hot as safer choices, including raw-milk cheese. Baking cheese into a hot casserole or pizza meets that step.

### Is aged raw-milk cheese OK?

CDC's chart lists any cheese made with raw (unpasteurized) milk as riskier, without an exception for aging. Choose pasteurized, or heat it until steaming hot.

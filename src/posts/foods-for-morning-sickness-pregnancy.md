**The short answer:** Try small amounts of food you can tolerate, often enough that your stomach is not empty. Dry toast or crackers, rice, pasta, fruit, and simple snacks are reasonable starting points. Sip fluids throughout the day. These ideas come from [ACOG's morning-sickness guidance](https://www.acog.org/womens-health/faqs/morning-sickness-nausea-and-vomiting-of-pregnancy) and the [NHS pregnancy guidance](https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/common-symptoms/vomiting-and-morning-sickness/); no single food works for everyone.

## What can I try first?

- **Before getting up:** ACOG suggests dry toast or crackers if waking with an empty stomach makes nausea worse.
- **During the day:** Try five or six small meals instead of a few large ones. A few bites of crackers, fruit, or nuts between meals may be easier than a full plate.
- **When flavors or smells are difficult:** The NHS suggests plain, lower-fat foods such as bread, rice, crackers, and pasta. Cold foods may be easier if cooking smells trigger nausea.
- **When you can add more variety:** ACOG suggests including some protein with meals. If they appeal to you, try nuts, nut butter, or pasteurized yogurt alongside a food you already tolerate.

These are options to test, not a required menu. If one food makes you feel worse, choose another. Pregnancy food-safety guidance still applies: use [pasteurized dairy](/blog/raw-milk-pregnancy/) and follow [safe preparation advice](/blog/food-safety-pregnancy/).

## What if drinking feels difficult?

Try small, frequent sips of water rather than drinking a large amount at once, as the [NHS advises](https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/common-symptoms/vomiting-and-morning-sickness/). ACOG recommends drinking throughout the day and notes that dehydration can worsen nausea. Our [water-intake guide](/blog/water-intake-pregnancy/) explains the usual pregnancy drinking-water range, but **keeping fluids down matters more than hitting a number when you are vomiting**.

**Call your pregnancy care team if you cannot keep liquids down, have very little or dark urine, or feel dizzy or faint when standing.** [ACOG lists these as dehydration warning signs](https://www.acog.org/womens-health/faqs/morning-sickness-nausea-and-vomiting-of-pregnancy). The [NHS also advises getting help](https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/common-symptoms/vomiting-and-morning-sickness/) for weight loss, abdominal pain, fever, or vomiting blood. You do not need to wait for a food strategy to work before asking for care.

## Do banana, ginger, or vitamin B6 treat morning sickness?

Bananas are one bland option, but a food's nutrient score does not show that it treats nausea. [ACOG discusses vitamin B6 as a treatment](https://www.acog.org/womens-health/faqs/morning-sickness-nausea-and-vomiting-of-pregnancy/), while our [B6 food guide](/blog/vitamin-b6-foods-pregnancy/) explains why a banana's B6 content is a separate nutrition question. [Ginger tea and ginger supplements](/blog/ginger-tea-capsules-pregnancy-nausea/) also differ in dose and evidence. Discuss supplements and medicines with your prenatal clinician, especially if symptoms affect your daily life.

PregNut's [food lookup](/food/) compares nutrients per 100 grams. It cannot tell you what your stomach will tolerate or whether vomiting needs treatment. Start with foods and fluids you can manage, then return to broader nutrition planning with your care team as you feel better.

**The short answer:** During pregnancy, cook steak to at least **145°F** in the thickest part, then let it rest for **3 minutes** before cutting or eating. That is the [CDC's safer choice for whole cuts of beef](https://www.cdc.gov/food-safety/foods/pregnant-women.html), and the CDC lists raw or undercooked meat as a riskier choice. Ground beef, including burgers, needs **160°F**.

## Why can't pregnant women eat rare or medium-rare steak?

Undercooked meat is one way people get *Toxoplasma*. The [CDC lists eating undercooked, contaminated meat](https://www.cdc.gov/toxoplasmosis/about/index.html) as a foodborne route of toxoplasmosis and names pregnant women among the people who need to be more cautious, because a mother newly infected during or just before pregnancy can pass the infection to her baby. The [FDA's pregnancy advice](https://www.fda.gov/food/people-risk-foodborne-illness/meat-poultry-seafood-food-safety-moms-be) uses the same targets: steaks, roasts, and chops to at least 145°F with a 3-minute rest.

## Is medium-rare or medium-well steak OK while pregnant?

"Rare," "medium-rare," and "medium-well" are descriptions, not a temperature reading. The [CDC says](https://www.cdc.gov/toxoplasmosis/prevention/index.html) you cannot tell whether meat is safely cooked by its color or texture. The question to ask is whether the thickest part reached **145°F** before resting. If you are cooking at home, use a food thermometer. At a restaurant, ask whether the kitchen can cook it to at least 145°F.

## What about burgers and ground beef?

Ground meat has a higher target. The [CDC chart](https://www.cdc.gov/food-safety/foods/pregnant-women.html) lists ground meats, such as beef and pork, cooked to **160°F**, and the CDC's toxoplasmosis page notes that ground meat does not need a rest time. A pink-centered burger is not a safety check either way; use a thermometer or ask the restaurant.

## Does freezing steak make it safe?

The [CDC says freezing meat](https://www.cdc.gov/toxoplasmosis/prevention/index.html) for several days at 0°F before cooking can greatly reduce the chance of *Toxoplasma* infection, but it also says freezing does not reliably kill other parasites and that **cooking to the recommended temperature is the safest method**.

## What nutrients does steak provide?

PregNut's historical USDA-based data lists [broiled top sirloin](/food/13930-beef-top-sirloin-steak-separable-lean-and-fat-broiled/) at **26.96 g protein**, **4.87 mg zinc**, **1.73 mg iron**, and **102.7 mg choline** per 100 g. [Grilled rib eye](/food/23197-beef-rib-eye-steak-boneless-lip-on-separable-lean-and-fat-cooked-grilled/) is listed at **23.69 g protein** and **5.91 mg zinc**. ## Common questions

### What if I ate a medium-rare steak while pregnant?

Tell your prenatal care team, especially if you develop symptoms. The [CDC says to see a doctor if you are pregnant and have a fever with other flu-like symptoms](https://www.cdc.gov/food-safety/signs-symptoms/index.html).

### Can I taste the meat while cooking?

The CDC's toxoplasmosis advice is not to sample meat until it is cooked.

### Is steak tartare or carpaccio OK?

No. Both are raw beef, which the CDC lists as a riskier choice during pregnancy.

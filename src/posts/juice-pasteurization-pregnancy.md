**The short answer:** Choose juice or cider that has been **pasteurized or otherwise treated to kill harmful bacteria**. The [CDC lists unpasteurized juice and cider](https://www.cdc.gov/food-safety/foods/pregnant-women.html) as riskier choices during pregnancy. “Fresh-squeezed” describes how a drink was made, not whether it was treated for safety.

## How can I check a bottled juice or cider?

Read the package for pasteurization or another treatment that kills harmful bacteria. The [FDA explains](https://www.fda.gov/food/buy-store-serve-safe-food/what-you-need-know-about-juice-safety) that untreated packaged juice must carry a warning about possible harmful bacteria. Juice can be sold from either a refrigerator or a shelf, so location alone is not the answer.

If you already have unpasteurized juice or cider, the [CDC's safer-choice table](https://www.cdc.gov/food-safety/foods/pregnant-women.html) says to bring it to a **rolling boil for at least one minute** before drinking. If a product's processing is unclear, choose one you can verify.

## What about juice made at a juice bar or farmers market?

Ask whether it was pasteurized or otherwise treated. The [FDA notes](https://www.fda.gov/food/people-risk-foodborne-illness/fruits-veggies-and-juices-food-safety-moms-be) that fresh-squeezed juice sold by the glass may be untreated and does **not** have to carry the warning label used on packaged untreated juice. Washing produce is still important, but it is not a way to establish that a finished juice was treated to kill bacteria.

## Are smoothies different?

A smoothie can contain juice. FDA advises checking that the juice used in a smoothie is pasteurized and that fresh fruits are washed. The word “smoothie” alone does not tell you how its ingredients were handled. Ask about the juice ingredient when ordering one, especially if it is prepared on site.

The [apple juice](/food/09016-apple-juice-canned-or-bottled-unsweetened/), [orange juice](/food/09207-orange-juice-canned-unsweetened/), and [raw grapefruit juice](/food/09128-grapefruit-juice-white-raw/) pages show nutrient amounts for reference foods. A name such as “canned” or “raw” in a nutrient database does not verify the processing or safety of the drink you buy.

**The short answer:** The [NIH recommended dietary allowance](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminB12-Consumer/) for vitamin B12 during pregnancy is **2.6 micrograms (mcg) a day**. Eggs and dairy can contribute if you eat them. Plants do not naturally provide B12, so a vegan diet needs a reliable fortified food or supplement source; discuss the full plan with your prenatal clinician.

## Compare foods without confusing 100 g with a serving

PregNut's [USDA-based food data](/about/) contains these B12 amounts **per 100 g**:

- [Hard-boiled egg](/food/01129-egg-hard-boiled/): **1.11 mcg**. One typical egg weighs less than 100 g; cook eggs thoroughly during pregnancy.
- [Plain low-fat yogurt](/food/01117-yogurt-plain-low-fat-12-grams-protein-per-8-ounce/): **0.56 mcg**. Choose pasteurized dairy products, following [CDC pregnancy food-safety guidance](https://www.cdc.gov/food-safety/foods/pregnant-women.html).
- [Soy milk with added nutrients](/food/16139-soymilk-original-and-vanilla-with-added-calcium-vitamins-a-and-d/): **0.85 mcg** in this database entry. Fortification differs by brand and can change, so the current carton label is more useful than this historical comparison.

NIH also lists some **fortified cereals and nutritional yeasts** as possible B12 sources. Check the Nutrition Facts panel for the actual B12 amount; the word “plant-based” by itself does not mean B12 is present.

## Is my prenatal enough?

Read the **serving size** and **B12 amount** on its Supplement Facts panel. A prenatal label, the [food lookup](/food/), and a list of your usual meals can help your care team assess your intake. If you eat no animal foods, or have a condition affecting B12 absorption, ask about testing or supplementation rather than relying on symptoms. The [NIH pregnancy review](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Pregnancy-HealthProfessional/) notes that vegetarian and vegan diets can be nutritionally adequate when planned carefully, with special attention to B12 and several other nutrients.

**The short answer:** You can get vitamin A from orange and green vegetables without eating liver. Sweet potatoes, carrots, and spinach contain **provitamin A carotenoids**, which the body converts to vitamin A. The [NIH pregnancy review](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Pregnancy-HealthProfessional/) lists **770 mcg retinol activity equivalents (RAE) per day** for pregnant adults and **750 mcg RAE** for pregnant teens.

## Three plant-food examples

The [NIH vitamin A food table](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminA-HealthProfessional/) gives these amounts for the specified portions:

| Food | Portion | Vitamin A |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| Sweet potato, baked in its skin | 1 whole | 1,403 mcg RAE |
| Spinach, frozen and boiled | ½ cup | 573 mcg RAE |
| Raw carrots | ½ cup | 459 mcg RAE |

The sweet potato amount is higher than the daily pregnancy recommendation. **That does not mean a baked sweet potato exceeds the safety limit.** The [NIH pregnancy review](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Pregnancy-HealthProfessional/) explains that the upper limit applies to **preformed vitamin A**, not the provitamin A carotenoids in these vegetables. The RAE unit allows different forms of vitamin A to be compared for intake; it does not make their safety limits interchangeable.

## Why is liver treated differently?

Liver contains a concentrated amount of **preformed vitamin A**. The NIH food table lists **6,582 mcg RAE in 3 ounces of pan-fried beef liver**, above the pregnancy upper limit for preformed vitamin A (**3,000 mcg for adults, 2,800 mcg for teens**). The [NHS advises avoiding liver and liver products during pregnancy](https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/keeping-well/foods-to-avoid/). Choose other foods for vitamin A and ask your prenatal clinician about your diet if you have a concern about intake.

## What should I check on my prenatal label?

Vitamin A may appear as **retinol, retinyl acetate, or retinyl palmitate** (preformed vitamin A), **beta-carotene** (provitamin A), or a mixture. A single total vitamin A number may not show how much is preformed. [NIH warns against high-dose preformed vitamin A supplements in pregnancy](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminA-Consumer/). Bring your [prenatal label](/blog/prenatal-vitamin-label-pregnancy/) and any other supplements to your clinician before adding a separate vitamin A product. Do not stop a prescribed prenatal based on the vitamin A number alone.

**The short answer:** Yes, you can eat lobster while pregnant when it is fully cooked. American and spiny lobster are on the [FDA's Best Choices list](https://www.fda.gov/food/consumers/advice-about-eating-fish) for mercury, and FDA's pregnancy advice is **2 to 3 servings a week** from that list, with one serving equal to **4 ounces**. Raw or undercooked shellfish is on [CDC's riskier list](https://www.cdc.gov/food-safety/foods/pregnant-women.html).

## How much lobster can I eat while pregnant?

FDA does not set a separate lobster limit. Lobster counts toward the same **2 to 3 servings a week** (4 ounces each) as other Best Choices such as [salmon](/blog/salmon-pregnancy/), [shrimp](/blog/shrimp-pregnancy/), and crab. A lobster tail one night and a salmon fillet another night would be two servings for the week.

## How do I know lobster is cooked?

The [FDA's seafood advice for moms-to-be](https://www.fda.gov/food/people-risk-foodborne-illness/meat-poultry-seafood-food-safety-moms-be) says the flesh of cooked lobster should be **opaque (milky white)**. FDA's [seafood handling page](https://www.fda.gov/food/buy-store-serve-safe-food/selecting-and-serving-fresh-and-frozen-seafood-safely) adds that cooked shrimp, scallops, crab, and lobster flesh becomes **firm, pearly, and opaque**. CDC's chart describes cooked shellfish the same way: pearly or white, and opaque. Boiled, steamed, grilled, and baked lobster all work once the meat reaches that point.

## Are lobster rolls safe during pregnancy?

A lobster roll made with fully cooked lobster fits the cooked-shellfish guidance, even served cold, if the meat was cooked first and kept refrigerated. [FDA says](https://www.fda.gov/food/buy-store-serve-safe-food/selecting-and-serving-fresh-and-frozen-seafood-safely) not to leave seafood out of the refrigerator for more than **2 hours** (1 hour above 90°F). For the mayonnaise, FDA [notes](https://www.fda.gov/food/people-risk-foodborne-illness/dairy-and-eggs-food-safety-moms-be) that commercial mayonnaise contains pasteurized eggs; a house-made mayo is the one to ask about. See our [mayonnaise guide](/blog/mayonnaise-pregnancy/). A warm, butter-style roll avoids the question.

## Can you eat raw lobster or lobster sashimi?

No. CDC lists **raw or undercooked fish or shellfish**, including sashimi, sushi, and ceviche, as riskier choices during pregnancy, and FDA says moms-to-be should avoid raw or undercooked shellfish. Choose lobster that is cooked until opaque.

## What about crab and crab legs?

Crab is also an FDA Best Choice and follows the same rules: cook until the flesh is pearly or white and opaque, and count it toward the same weekly servings. Our [shrimp guide](/blog/shrimp-pregnancy/) has a crab section, including crab legs and crab cakes.

## What nutrients does lobster provide?

PregNut's historical USDA-based data lists [cooked northern lobster](/food/15148-lobster-northern-cooked-moist-heat/) at **19 g protein**, **1.43 mcg vitamin B12**, and **80.9 mg choline** per 100 g. [FDA lists](https://www.fda.gov/food/consumers/advice-about-eating-fish) omega-3 fats, iron, iodine, and choline among the nutrients fish provide during pregnancy, and its fish chart includes lobster. Lobster has less DHA than salmon in PregNut's data, so see our [salmon guide](/blog/salmon-pregnancy/) if DHA is your goal.

## Common questions

### Can I eat lobster in the first trimester?

Yes. FDA's fish advice applies to the whole pregnancy and does not set different amounts by trimester.

### How fresh does lobster need to be?

[FDA says](https://www.fda.gov/food/buy-store-serve-safe-food/selecting-and-serving-fresh-and-frozen-seafood-safely) live lobsters should show some leg movement, and that they spoil rapidly after death, so only live lobsters should be selected and prepared. Cooked lobster from a store should be kept cold until you eat it.

### What if I ate undercooked lobster while pregnant?

Tell your prenatal care team what you ate and when. The [CDC says pregnant women should see a doctor](https://www.cdc.gov/food-safety/signs-symptoms/index.html) if they have a fever and other flu-like symptoms.

**The short answer:** Salami and prosciutto are fine during pregnancy when they are **heated to 165°F or until steaming hot**. [CDC's pregnancy food-safety chart](https://www.cdc.gov/food-safety/foods/pregnant-women.html) lists **unheated** deli meat, cold cuts, and **fermented or dry sausages** as riskier choices, and the same foods heated to 165°F or until steaming hot as safer choices. Cold slices on a sandwich or charcuterie board are the unheated version.

## Can I eat salami while pregnant?

Yes, if it is heated first. Salami is a fermented or dry sausage, the category CDC names on its riskier list when unheated. The [CDC explains](https://www.cdc.gov/listeria/causes/index.html) that *Listeria* can grow in refrigerated foods and is killed by heating, and its [Listeria prevention page](https://www.cdc.gov/listeria/prevention/index.html) lists pregnant women among the groups at higher risk. Salami baked on a pizza, crisped in a pan, or cooked into a pasta sauce until steaming hot fits the safer column.

## Is prosciutto safe during pregnancy?

CDC's chart does not name prosciutto. It is a cured ham that is usually sliced thin and eaten cold, like other cold cuts, so the same rule applies: heat it to **165°F or until steaming hot**. Prosciutto is cured rather than cooked, and the [FDA notes](https://www.fda.gov/food/people-risk-foodborne-illness/ready-eat-foods-food-safety-moms-be) that the *Toxoplasma* parasite can be found in raw or undercooked pork. Prosciutto crisped in the oven, baked on a pizza, or cooked into a dish is the safer way to eat it. Parma ham is a type of prosciutto and follows the same rule.

## Can you eat fried or cooked salami while pregnant?

Yes. Fried salami, salami on a hot pizza, and salami cooked in a casserole all fit CDC's safer choice once the meat is **steaming hot** throughout. Check that the slices are hot, not just warm, and eat them soon after cooking.

## What about charcuterie boards and salami sandwiches?

A cold charcuterie board and a cold salami or Italian sub are unheated, which puts them in CDC's riskier column. If you want a sandwich, heat it until the meat is steaming hot, for example a toasted or pressed sandwich. Cheese on the board needs its own check; see our [soft cheese guide](/blog/soft-cheese-pregnancy/). Our [deli meat guide](/blog/deli-meat-pregnancy/) covers turkey, ham, and other cold cuts, and the [pepperoni guide](/blog/pepperoni-pregnancy/) covers pizza and snack sticks.

## Is sausage different from salami?

It depends on the type. Dry or fermented sausages, such as salami and pepperoni, follow CDC's heat-to-165°F rule. Fresh raw sausages are ground meat: CDC says to cook ground meats such as beef and pork to **160°F**, and ground chicken and turkey to **165°F**, checked with a food thermometer.

## What nutrients does salami provide?

Mostly protein, fat, and calories. PregNut's historical USDA-based data lists [dry or hard salami](/food/07072-salami-dry-or-hard-pork-beef/) at **21.07 g protein** and **378 calories** per 100 g. Our [iron foods guide](/blog/iron-foods-pregnancy/) lists other protein foods that also add iron.

## Common questions

### What if I ate cold salami or prosciutto while pregnant?

Eating it once does not mean you have an infection. Tell your prenatal care team what you ate and when. The [CDC says pregnant women should call the doctor right away](https://www.cdc.gov/food-safety/signs-symptoms/index.html) if they have a fever and feel more tired and achy than usual.

### Does "uncured" or "nitrate-free" salami change the advice?

No. CDC's guidance is about **fermented or dry sausages** and cold cuts in general, and those label terms do not tell you the product was heated before you eat it.

### Does it matter which trimester I am in?

CDC's chart does not set different rules by trimester. The heat-it-first advice applies throughout pregnancy.

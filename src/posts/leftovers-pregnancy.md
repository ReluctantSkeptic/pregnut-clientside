**The short answer:** Cooked leftovers can fit into a pregnancy diet when they were stored promptly and reheated thoroughly. [FDA's pregnancy food-safety guidance](https://www.fda.gov/food/people-risk-foodborne-illness/cooking-food-safety-moms-be) says to reheat leftovers to **165°F (74°C)**. A food thermometer is more reliable than judging the center by touch or by steam at the edge of a dish.

## How soon should I refrigerate leftovers?

[FDA advises refrigerating or freezing prepared food and leftovers within two hours](https://www.fda.gov/food/people-risk-foodborne-illness/ready-eat-foods-food-safety-moms-be). If the surrounding temperature is above **90°F (32°C)**, the limit is **one hour**. Keep the refrigerator at **40°F (4°C) or below**. If perishable food stayed out longer, discard it rather than relying on reheating to undo unsafe storage.

For restaurant leftovers, [FDA advises going directly home and refrigerating them promptly](https://www.fda.gov/food/people-risk-foodborne-illness/eating-out-bringing-food-safety-moms-be). If you cannot get home within two hours of being served, leave the leftovers behind.

## How many days can I keep them?

The [FoodSafety.gov cold storage chart](https://www.foodsafety.gov/food-safety-charts/cold-food-storage-charts) gives **three to four days** in the refrigerator for many cooked leftovers, including cooked meat or poultry, soups, stews, and pizza. This is a general limit for food that was refrigerated promptly at 40°F or below; check the chart or product directions for foods with a different limit. Labeling the container with the date makes the decision easier.

## How do I reheat a meal evenly?

Reheat leftovers to **165°F throughout**. [FDA notes that microwaves can leave cold spots](https://www.fda.gov/food/people-risk-foodborne-illness/cooking-food-safety-moms-be), so cover and stir or rotate the food, allow any instructed standing time, and check the temperature in more than one place. Bring leftover sauces, soups, and gravies to a boil.

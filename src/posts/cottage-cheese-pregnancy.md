**The short answer:** Yes, you can eat cottage cheese while pregnant when it is **made with pasteurized milk**. [CDC's pregnancy food-safety chart](https://www.cdc.gov/food-safety/foods/pregnant-women.html) names **cottage cheese**, along with cream cheese, string cheese, feta, and mozzarella, as a safer choice when made with pasteurized milk. Cheese made from **unpasteurized (raw) milk** is on CDC's riskier list.

## Is cottage cheese a soft cheese to avoid?

No, not when it is pasteurized. Cottage cheese is a fresh, soft cheese, which is why it shows up on some "avoid soft cheese" lists. CDC's riskier column is about soft cheese made from **unpasteurized milk** and queso fresco-type cheeses, while [CDC's Listeria prevention page](https://www.cdc.gov/listeria/prevention/index.html) lists pasteurized cottage cheese among the choices to make instead. Queso fresco, queso blanco, and requesón are different: CDC lists them as riskier unless heated, even when pasteurized; see our [queso fresco guide](/blog/queso-fresco-pregnancy/).

## What cottage cheese can I eat while pregnant?

Choose cottage cheese whose label says **made with pasteurized milk** or lists pasteurized milk in the ingredients. The [FDA notes](https://www.fda.gov/food/people-risk-foodborne-illness/dairy-and-eggs-food-safety-moms-be) that milk sold across state lines is pasteurized, but some dairy products are not, especially those made and sold locally on dairy farms or at local cheese stores. PregNut does not verify brands, so check the tub you buy.

## How should I store cottage cheese?

The [FDA advises](https://www.fda.gov/food/people-risk-foodborne-illness/dairy-and-eggs-food-safety-moms-be) keeping milk and milk products refrigerated at **40°F** or below and following "use-by" dates. FDA also notes that *Listeria* [can grow at refrigerator temperatures](https://www.fda.gov/food/people-risk-foodborne-illness/ready-eat-foods-food-safety-moms-be) and advises using ready-to-eat perishable foods, including dairy, as soon as possible. Put the tub back in the fridge after serving rather than leaving it out; FDA's rule is within **2 hours** (1 hour above 90°F).

## Is cottage cheese good to eat during pregnancy?

It is a high-protein dairy food. PregNut's historical USDA-based data lists [creamed cottage cheese](/food/01012-cheese-cottage-creamed-large-or-small-curd/) at **11.12 g protein**, **83 mg calcium**, and **0.43 mcg vitamin B12** per 100 g, at **98 calories**. For calcium, other dairy foods list more: the [NIH calcium fact sheet](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Calcium-HealthProfessional/) lists **415 mg** in 8 ounces of plain low-fat yogurt and **333 mg** in 1.5 ounces of part-skim mozzarella, against a pregnancy RDA of **1,000 mg a day** for ages 19 to 50. Our [yogurt guide](/blog/yogurt-pregnancy/) and [vegetarian protein guide](/blog/vegetarian-protein-pregnancy/) compare more options.

## What about mozzarella and string cheese?

The same rule applies. CDC names mozzarella and string cheese on its safer list **when made with pasteurized milk**. Fresh mozzarella from a specialty shop or farm deserves the same label question as any local cheese. Melted on a pizza or baked in lasagna, pasteurized soft cheese heated to **165°F or until steaming hot** is also on [CDC's safer list](https://www.cdc.gov/food-safety/foods/pregnant-women.html). PregNut lists [part-skim mozzarella](/food/01028-cheese-mozzarella-part-skim-milk/) at **782 mg calcium** and **24.26 g protein** per 100 g.

## Common questions

### Is cottage cheese safe in the first trimester?

CDC's chart does not set different cheese rules by trimester. Pasteurized cottage cheese is on its safer list in the first, second, and third trimesters.

### Can I eat cottage cheese in cooked dishes?

Yes. Pasteurized cottage cheese baked into lasagna, pancakes, or a casserole fits CDC's safer list, and heating soft cheese until steaming hot adds a margin.

### What if I ate unpasteurized cottage cheese?

An exposure does not mean you have an infection. Tell your prenatal care team what you ate. For *Listeria*, [CDC says](https://www.cdc.gov/food-safety/signs-symptoms/index.html) pregnant women should call the doctor right away if they have a fever and feel more tired and achy than usual.

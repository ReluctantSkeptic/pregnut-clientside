**The short answer:** Fish can be part of a pregnancy diet. [FDA and EPA](https://www.fda.gov/food/consumers/advice-about-eating-fish) recommend two to three adult servings per week from their lower-mercury **Best Choices** category, with an adult serving defined as 4 ounces. Select a variety and cook seafood thoroughly.

## How do I choose fish?

Start with the current [FDA/EPA fish chart](https://www.fda.gov/food/consumers/advice-about-eating-fish), because mercury guidance depends on the species. Salmon, sardines, shrimp, and canned light tuna are examples of Best Choices. Albacore (white) tuna is a **Good Choice**, for which FDA/EPA advise one serving in a week and no other fish that week. Bigeye tuna is in **Choices to Avoid**. The word “tuna” alone is not specific enough.

## What about nutrient rankings?

PregNut's [food lookup](/food/) compares nutrients per **100 g**; it does not rank fish for mercury or food safety. A nutrient-dense fish may still be a poor choice for pregnancy if it has high mercury. Use the FDA/EPA chart first, then compare nutrients among suitable foods.

## Common questions

### Is salmon safe during pregnancy?

Salmon is a Best Choice on the FDA/EPA chart. Cook it thoroughly and count it toward the weekly fish guidance. Check local advisories for fish you or someone you know caught.

### Is canned light tuna the same as albacore?

No. Canned light tuna is a Best Choice; albacore is a Good Choice with a different weekly limit. Read the can label to identify the type.

### Can I eat raw sushi while pregnant?

The [FDA food safety guidance](https://www.fda.gov/food/people-risk-foodborne-illness/meat-poultry-seafood-food-safety-moms-be) warns that raw fish can carry parasites or bacteria. Choose thoroughly cooked seafood.

### Does cooking remove mercury?

No. FDA explains that mercury is distributed through fish tissue, so cooking does not remove it. Choose lower-mercury species instead.

### What if I ate a fish on the avoid list once?

FDA says a single serving is not a reason to panic; choose lower-mercury fish going forward. If you have a specific exposure concern, contact your prenatal clinician.

**The short answer:** The pregnancy recommended dietary allowance for vitamin D is **15 mcg (600 IU) daily**. Few foods naturally contain much vitamin D. Fatty fish can help; fortified milk, plant drinks, and some cereals may also contribute. Check the current package label because fortification varies. These points come from the [NIH vitamin D fact sheet](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminD-Consumer/) and [ACOG's pregnancy guide](https://www.acog.org/womens-health/faqs/healthy-eating-during-pregnancy).

## Compare the food, then check your portion

PregNut lists vitamin D in **IU per 100 g**, which is a consistent comparison amount rather than a suggested serving:

- [Cooked sockeye salmon](/food/15086-salmon-sockeye-cooked-dry-heat/): **670 IU**. If eating fish, follow the [FDA/EPA pregnancy fish advice](https://www.fda.gov/food/consumers/advice-about-eating-fish) for species and weekly amounts, and cook it thoroughly.
- [Soy milk with added vitamins A and D](/food/16139-soymilk-original-and-vanilla-with-added-calcium-vitamins-a-and-d/): **43 IU** in this particular database entry. Another brand may have a different amount; its label is the better source for what is in your glass.
- [Hard-boiled egg](/food/01129-egg-hard-boiled/): **87 IU**. The 100 g value covers more than one typical egg, so do not read it as an amount per egg.

Use [PregNut's food lookup](/food/) for other comparisons. Vitamin D works with [calcium from foods and supplements](/blog/calcium-without-dairy-pregnancy/) to support bone health, but the chart cannot measure your vitamin D status.

## Should I add a vitamin D supplement?

First check the **serving size and vitamin D amount** on your prenatal label. If your clinician suspects deficiency, they can decide whether testing or an additional supplement makes sense. [NIH's pregnancy review](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Pregnancy-HealthProfessional/) notes that professional recommendations on extra vitamin D differ and that evidence for preventing pregnancy complications is mixed. Do not treat a larger dose as a proven way to prevent those outcomes.

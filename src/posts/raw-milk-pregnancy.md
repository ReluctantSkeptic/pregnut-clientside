**The short answer:** Choose **pasteurized milk and dairy products made from pasteurized milk** during pregnancy. [CDC lists raw (unpasteurized) milk and products made from it as riskier choices](https://www.cdc.gov/food-safety/foods/pregnant-women.html). Pasteurization kills harmful germs; a farm source, refrigeration, or a claim that milk is “natural” does not establish the same protection.

## Why does pasteurization matter?

[CDC explains](https://www.cdc.gov/food-safety/foods/raw-milk.html) that raw milk can carry germs including *Listeria*, *E. coli*, and *Salmonella*. Pregnancy increases the risk of serious illness from some foodborne germs. Pasteurized milk offers the nutritional benefits of milk without the added raw-milk risk; you do not need raw milk to obtain its nutrients.

## How do I check milk or yogurt?

1. **Read the package:** Look for a statement that the milk is pasteurized. For yogurt or another dairy product, check that it was made with pasteurized milk. [FDA advises checking dairy labels](https://www.fda.gov/food/people-risk-foodborne-illness/dairy-and-eggs-food-safety-moms-be).
2. **Ask when there is no package:** At a farm stand, café, or market, ask whether the milk used was pasteurized. If you cannot confirm, choose another option.
3. **Keep it cold:** FDA advises refrigerating milk and milk products at **40°F (4°C) or colder** and following the product's date instructions. Pasteurization does not replace safe storage.

PregNut's [fortified nonfat milk](/food/42290-milk-fluid-nonfat-calcium-fortified-fat-free-or-skim/) and [plain yogurt](/food/01116-yogurt-plain-whole-milk-8-grams-protein-per-8-ounce/) pages compare nutrients in **100 g reference entries**. Those entries do not verify the processing, brand, storage, or safety of a product you buy. Check its actual label before using a nutrient chart to choose it.

## Does the same rule cover cheese?

Milk processing is only part of the cheese question. CDC gives additional advice for certain soft cheeses, including queso fresco-type cheese even when made with pasteurized milk. See our [cheese and pregnancy guide](/blog/soft-cheese-pregnancy/) for those distinctions.

If you drank raw milk and feel ill or have a specific exposure concern, contact your prenatal clinician. [FDA describes symptoms and pregnancy concerns related to listeriosis](https://www.fda.gov/food/health-educators/listeria-food-safety-moms-be); this page cannot assess an individual exposure.

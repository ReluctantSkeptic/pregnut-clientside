**The short answer:** Yes, you can eat tilapia and cod while pregnant. Both are on the [FDA/EPA "Best Choices" list](https://www.fda.gov/food/consumers/advice-about-eating-fish), the fish lowest in mercury. FDA's pregnancy advice is **2 to 3 servings a week** from that list, with **1 serving = 4 ounces**. Cook the fish to **145°F**, or until the flesh is opaque and separates easily with a fork, as [CDC advises](https://www.cdc.gov/food-safety/foods/pregnant-women.html) for fish in pregnancy.

## How much tilapia or cod can I eat while pregnant?

Count tilapia and cod toward one weekly total. The [FDA/EPA advice](https://www.fda.gov/food/consumers/advice-about-eating-fish) is 2 to 3 four-ounce servings a week from the Best Choices list, and the Dietary Guidelines figure it cites is **8 to 12 ounces of a variety of seafood a week** in pregnancy, from choices lower in mercury. Mixing tilapia and cod with other Best Choices, such as [salmon](/blog/salmon-pregnancy/) and [shrimp](/blog/shrimp-pregnancy/), fits that advice.

For fish caught by family or friends, [FDA says](https://www.fda.gov/food/consumers/advice-about-eating-fish) to check local fish advisories; if there is no advisory, eat only one serving and no other fish that week.

## Why is fish recommended during pregnancy?

The [FDA says](https://www.fda.gov/food/consumers/advice-about-eating-fish) fish intake during pregnancy is recommended because **moderate scientific evidence** shows it can help a baby's cognitive development. FDA lists omega-3 fats (DHA and EPA), iron, iodine, and choline among the nutrients fish provide. Our [lower-mercury fish guide](/blog/low-mercury-fish-pregnancy/) covers the full chart.

## How should I cook tilapia or cod?

Any method works if the center is done. [CDC lists fish cooked to 145°F](https://www.cdc.gov/food-safety/foods/pregnant-women.html), or until the flesh is opaque and separates easily with a fork, as the safer choice. The [FDA's version](https://www.fda.gov/food/people-risk-foodborne-illness/meat-poultry-seafood-food-safety-moms-be) is the same: cook fish until it is opaque (milky white) and flakes with a fork.

- **Baked, broiled, or grilled:** check the thickest part.
- **Fried or breaded:** a crisp coating does not tell you the center is cooked; check the flesh.
- **Frozen fillets and fish sticks:** follow the package directions and check the center.

[FDA also advises](https://www.fda.gov/food/people-risk-foodborne-illness/meat-poultry-seafood-food-safety-moms-be) not reusing a marinade that touched raw seafood unless you boil it first.

## Is smoked cod or raw fish OK?

Not as is. The [FDA lists cod](https://www.fda.gov/food/people-risk-foodborne-illness/ready-eat-foods-food-safety-moms-be) among the refrigerated smoked seafoods to avoid unless they are in a cooked dish, such as a casserole. CDC lists **raw or undercooked fish**, including sashimi, sushi, and ceviche, as riskier, and **canned fish** as safer. See our [smoked salmon guide](/blog/smoked-salmon-pregnancy/) for the label check.

## What nutrients do tilapia and cod provide?

PregNut's historical USDA-based data lists [cooked tilapia](/food/15262-tilapia-cooked-dry-heat/) at **26.15 g protein**, **1.86 mcg vitamin B12**, and **0.13 g DHA** per 100 g, and [cooked Atlantic cod](/food/15016-cod-atlantic-cooked-dry-heat/) at **22.83 g protein**, **83.7 mg choline**, and **0.15 g DHA** per 100 g. For iodine, the [NIH iodine fact sheet](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Iodine-HealthProfessional/) lists **146 mcg** in 3 ounces of baked cod; the pregnancy RDA is **220 mcg** a day.

## Common questions

### Is tilapia bad for pregnancy?

No. FDA's chart lists tilapia as a Best Choice, with no separate entry for farmed or wild tilapia. Cook it through and count it toward your weekly fish servings.

### Is tilapia or cod safe in the first trimester?

FDA's fish advice and CDC's chart do not change by trimester. The same serving and cooking guidance applies throughout pregnancy.

### What if I ate undercooked fish?

Tell your prenatal care team what you ate. [CDC says](https://www.cdc.gov/food-safety/signs-symptoms/index.html) to see a doctor if you are pregnant and have a fever and other flu-like symptoms.

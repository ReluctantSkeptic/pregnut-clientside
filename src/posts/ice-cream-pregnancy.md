**The short answer:** Yes, you can eat ice cream while pregnant. The [CDC lists pasteurized milk, yogurt, and ice cream as safer choices](https://www.cdc.gov/listeria/prevention/index.html) and raw-milk ice cream as riskier. The [FDA advises avoiding homemade ice cream](https://www.fda.gov/food/people-risk-foodborne-illness/dairy-and-eggs-food-safety-moms-be) made with raw eggs; store-bought ice cream is the simpler choice.

## Is store-bought ice cream safe during pregnancy?

Yes, when it is made with pasteurized milk. The FDA notes that store-bought versions of foods that traditionally use raw eggs are often made with cooked or pasteurized eggs. Keep it frozen until you serve it, and check for recalls: the [CDC explains](https://www.cdc.gov/listeria/causes/index.html) that *Listeria* can spread inside food-processing facilities and grow in cold food, so a pasteurized label does not rule out contamination after processing. The FDA advises returning or discarding recalled food.

## What ice cream should you avoid while pregnant?

- **Homemade ice cream made with raw eggs.** FDA lists it among homemade foods to avoid. Use pasteurized eggs, or a custard base cooked to 160°F, the CDC's target for egg dishes without meat.
- **Ice cream made from raw (unpasteurized) milk**, for example from a farm stand. The CDC lists raw-milk ice cream as a riskier choice.
- **Recalled products.** Check the recall notice before eating ice cream from your freezer if a brand is in the news.

## Is store-bought cookie dough ice cream safe?

Yes. The FDA's pregnancy page answers this directly: store-bought cookie dough ice cream is safe to eat. Raw homemade cookie dough is a different story, because the CDC lists raw cookie dough and raw batter as riskier choices.

## Can pregnant women eat soft serve?

The CDC and FDA pregnancy pages do not single out soft serve. The same check applies: soft serve made from a **pasteurized** dairy mix fits the CDC's safer-choice row for pasteurized ice cream. If you are unsure at a small shop, ask whether the mix is pasteurized.

## Does ice cream have any pregnancy nutrients?

It adds some calcium and calories. PregNut's historical USDA-based data lists [vanilla ice cream](/food/19095-ice-creams-vanilla/) at **128 mg calcium**, **3.5 g protein**, and **207 calories** per 100 g. [ACOG's morning sickness advice](https://www.acog.org/womens-health/faqs/morning-sickness-nausea-and-vomiting-of-pregnancy) lists dairy foods, such as milk, ice cream, and yogurt, among nonmeat protein options if meat is hard to face.

## Common questions

### Is vanilla ice cream OK in the first trimester?

Yes. The CDC's food-safety chart does not change by trimester. See our [morning sickness food guide](/blog/foods-for-morning-sickness-pregnancy/) if nausea is shaping what you can eat.

### What about gelato or frozen yogurt?

Apply the same check: made with pasteurized milk and stored frozen. Our [milk and yogurt label guide](/blog/raw-milk-pregnancy/) explains the wording to look for.

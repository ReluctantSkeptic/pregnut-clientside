**The short answer:** Check the milk first. [CDC's pregnancy food-safety chart](https://www.cdc.gov/food-safety/foods/pregnant-women.html) lists **soft cheese made from unpasteurized (raw) milk, including blue-veined cheese**, as a riskier choice. [FDA says](https://www.fda.gov/food/people-risk-foodborne-illness/fact-or-fiction-food-safety-moms-be) not to eat blue-veined cheeses unless they are made with pasteurized milk, and CDC lists **pasteurized soft cheeses heated to 165°F or until steaming hot** as a safer choice.

## Why is blue cheese a concern during pregnancy?

The concern is *Listeria*. The [FDA explains](https://www.fda.gov/food/people-risk-foodborne-illness/fact-or-fiction-food-safety-moms-be) that cheese made from unpasteurized milk can become contaminated with *Listeria*, which can be harmful to a pregnant woman and her baby, and that some soft cheeses may be made with unpasteurized milk. CDC's [Listeria prevention page](https://www.cdc.gov/listeria/prevention/index.html) lists pregnant women among the groups at higher risk.

## Is pasteurized blue cheese safe while pregnant?

The answer depends on the guidance you use and how it is served. FDA's advice is that blue-veined cheese is acceptable when the label says **"made with pasteurized milk."** CDC's chart does not list blue cheese by name on its safer side; it lists **pasteurized soft cheese heated to 165°F or until steaming hot**. If you want to follow the more cautious option, eat blue cheese melted or cooked until steaming hot. Ask your prenatal clinician if you are unsure about a specific cheese.

## Can you eat cooked blue cheese, like on a burger?

Yes, when it is heated until **steaming hot**. Blue cheese melted into a hot sauce, baked into a dish, or melted on a burger fits CDC's heated-soft-cheese option. A cold crumble added after cooking has not been heated. Cook the burger itself to **160°F**, CDC's temperature for ground beef; see our [steak and burger guide](/blog/medium-rare-steak-pregnancy/).

## Is blue cheese dressing safe during pregnancy?

For store-bought dressing, check the label for pasteurized milk or cheese. On the egg side, [FDA notes](https://www.fda.gov/food/people-risk-foodborne-illness/dairy-and-eggs-food-safety-moms-be) that commercial mayonnaise, dressings, and sauces contain pasteurized eggs. House-made blue cheese dressing at a restaurant is the one to ask about, both for the cheese and for homemade mayonnaise. Our [ranch and salad dressing guide](/blog/ranch-dressing-pregnancy/) covers bottled and restaurant dressings, and PregNut has [blue cheese dressing](/food/04539-salad-dressing-blue-or-roquefort-cheese-commercial-regular/) nutrition data.

## What about blue cheese crumbles, Roquefort, and Gorgonzola?

Use the same label check for each one. The name of a cheese does not tell you whether its milk was pasteurized, and [FDA notes](https://www.fda.gov/food/people-risk-foodborne-illness/dairy-and-eggs-food-safety-moms-be) that some cheeses, especially local or specialty ones, are not. If the package or the server cannot confirm pasteurized milk, skip it or choose a dish where the cheese is cooked until steaming hot.

## What nutrients does blue cheese provide?

PregNut's historical USDA-based data lists [blue cheese](/food/01004-cheese-blue/) at **528 mg calcium** and **21.4 g protein** per 100 g. A typical portion is much smaller than 100 g.

## Common questions

### What if I ate blue cheese while pregnant?

An exposure does not mean you have an infection. Tell your prenatal care team what you ate, and whether it was pasteurized if you know. [CDC says](https://www.cdc.gov/food-safety/signs-symptoms/index.html) pregnant women should call the doctor right away if they have a fever and feel more tired and achy than usual.

### Does the trimester change the advice?

CDC's chart does not set different cheese rules by trimester. The pasteurization and heating checks apply throughout pregnancy.

**The short answer:** You can get zinc from beans, nuts, seeds, whole grains, dairy, eggs, meat, and seafood. If you avoid meat, include a variety of zinc-containing foods and check your prenatal label. The [NIH pregnancy supplement review](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Pregnancy-HealthProfessional/) lists **11 mg per day** as the zinc recommended dietary allowance during pregnancy for adults ages 19–50 and **12 mg** for pregnant teens ages 14–18. A food ranking cannot tell how much zinc your body absorbs.

## Which meat-free foods contain zinc?

PregNut's [USDA-based food data](/about/) reports these amounts **per 100 g of the food as named**:

| Food | Zinc per 100 g | Preparation matters |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| [Roasted pumpkin and squash seed kernels](/food/12516-seeds-pumpkin-and-squash-seed-kernels-roasted/) | **7.64 mg** | This is 100 g of seeds, much more than a small handful. |
| [Boiled lentils](/food/16070-lentils-mature-seeds-boiled/) | **1.27 mg** | This is cooked lentils, not dry lentils. |
| [Cooked oats made with water](/food/08121-cereals-oats-regular-and-quick-unenriched-cooked-with-water-includes-boiling-and-microwaving/) | **1.00 mg** | Fortified packaged oatmeal may differ. |
| [Plain nonfat Greek yogurt](/food/01256-yogurt-greek-nonfat/) | **0.52 mg** | Choose pasteurized dairy products. |

These are equal-weight comparisons, **not equal everyday servings**. The [NIH zinc food table](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Zinc-HealthProfessional/) gives useful portion examples: **1 ounce roasted pumpkin seeds has about 2.2 mg**, **½ cup boiled lentils about 1.3 mg**, and **1 cup cooked oats about 2.3 mg**. Product and preparation differences can change the amount.

## Does plant zinc count the same as zinc from meat?

Plant foods do contribute zinc, but the amount listed in a food table is not the amount absorbed. [NIH explains](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Zinc-HealthProfessional/) that phytates in beans, nuts, and whole grains can reduce zinc absorption. That is one reason a vegetarian or vegan pregnancy diet deserves a review of the **whole diet**, not just a sum of food-table milligrams. PregNut does not estimate individual zinc absorption.

Fortified breakfast cereals can also supply zinc, but amounts depend on the brand and serving. Check the current package label rather than applying an older database value to the product you buy. For additional meat-free meal planning, see our [vegetarian protein](/blog/vegetarian-protein-pregnancy/), [iron](/blog/iron-foods-pregnancy/), and [vitamin B12](/blog/vegetarian-b12-pregnancy/) guides.

## Should I add a separate zinc supplement?

Check the **serving size and zinc amount** on your prenatal's Supplement Facts panel first. NIH notes that most prenatal supplements include zinc. For pregnant adults, NIH lists **40 mg per day** as the upper limit from food and supplements combined; for pregnant teens, the limit is **34 mg per day**. More zinc is not automatically better. [NIH says the effects of zinc supplementation on pregnancy outcomes are unclear](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Pregnancy-HealthProfessional/), and WHO does not recommend it as part of routine pregnancy care. Discuss a separate product with your clinician, especially if you avoid animal foods or have a condition that affects absorption.

## Common questions

### Are pumpkin seeds enough zinc for a day?

An ounce of roasted seeds contributes about **2.2 mg**, according to NIH. That is useful, but it is only part of the adult pregnancy **11 mg daily reference**. Other foods and your prenatal may contribute as well.

### Does a high-zinc food ranking mean I should eat that food?

No. Rankings compare nutrients, not food safety, your diet, or a suitable serving. For example, oysters can be very high in zinc, but [CDC advises avoiding raw or undercooked seafood during pregnancy](https://www.cdc.gov/food-safety/foods/pregnant-women.html). Check preparation guidance before choosing a food.

### Should vegetarians take more zinc than the label recommends?

Not by default. NIH notes that vegetarian and vegan diets may provide less absorbable zinc and advises discussing whether supplementation is needed with a health professional. Bring your usual foods and prenatal label to that conversation.

What is PregNut?

PregNut is a practical food lookup and planning tool for pregnancy. It shows which foods deliver more—or less—of the nutrients that matter most, using one consistent portion size so comparisons stay meaningful.

General food lists are easy to find. Understanding which specific foods help you reach a nutrient target is harder. PregNut brings that detail into one calm, approachable place while keeping the underlying evidence visible.

Evidence first

Recommendations prioritize nutrients with broad consensus across trusted medical and public health sources.

How to use PregNut

Top Foods

The Top Foods page compares foods across the nutrients in PregNut's database, broken down by food group.

How to use PregNut

Search

Enter a food name in the search bar. Choose a suggestion to open that food's nutrition page, or submit your search to browse all matching foods.

Each food page shows how 100 grams of the food compares with the site's nutrient reference amounts.

All foods use 100 gram (3.5 oz) portions for an apples-to-oranges comparison that stays consistent. The visual guide below shows what that looks like for common foods.

Suggestions show a limited set of matches. Submit your search to see more results, or try a narrower food name.

Is it based on science?

The nutrients

PregNut uses published nutrient targets and food-composition data to make comparisons. The targets and food amounts have different sources, and a food's nutrient score is not a medical recommendation to eat it.

The original nutrient selection drew on pregnancy nutrition guidance and research. See the editorial approach below for how the newer guides cite their sources.

Disclaimer

Use as a starting point.

PregNut is created with the intention to be a starting point for some of your pregnancy nutrition research. It is not meant to replace your doctor or nutritionist, nor to diagnose, cure, or treat any disease, and certainly not to be the final word on any nutrition science.

The food-composition values in this version come from static project files derived from the USDA National Nutrient Database for Standard Reference. See USDA's SR Legacy data archive for the official dataset. The project's files do not record the exact USDA release for each row. Food names were cleaned up for readability, and values describe 100 grams rather than a specific brand or prepared portion.

This is a fixed historical snapshot, not a live USDA feed. USDA says the final Standard Reference Legacy release was in 2018; newer Foundation Foods and Branded Foods data may differ. Check current product labels for packaged foods and use these charts for comparison rather than exact current-product values.

Who and how

How PregNut's guides are made

Yuriy Stasyuk publishes PregNut. His background is in healthcare data analysis and reporting, as described in his Washington Health Alliance biography. That experience informs the site's food-data comparisons; it is not a substitute for prenatal clinical expertise. Article bylines identify who published each guide. A named medical review is shown only if one has actually taken place.

The food pages use the site's USDA-based data to compare nutrients in 100 grams of food. The pregnancy nutrition guides connect selected comparisons to questions readers ask, with links to the original public-health and clinical sources used for pregnancy guidance. Food-composition numbers, recommended intakes, and food-safety advice should be read as separate kinds of information.

Some guides were drafted with AI assistance and edited before publication. Source links let readers inspect the underlying guidance. The articles do not replace advice from an obstetric clinician or registered dietitian; a publication date or byline does not imply independent clinical review.

When a guide receives a material update, its updated date appears beside the publication date. Guidance can change, so check the linked source for its latest version.

Found an incorrect value, outdated source, or unclear statement? Email ystasyuk@gmail.com with the page URL and the issue so it can be checked.
